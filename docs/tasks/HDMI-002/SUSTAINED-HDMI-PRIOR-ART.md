# Sustained lower-resolution HDMI on ESP32-P4

**November 22 research result.** The strongest same-board moving-picture precedent is WLED-MM on the Olimex P4-PC. Its author reports working 720×576 and 800×600 timings. RetroESP32-P4 selects a real 640×480 output for games. Both keep a complete output image in memory. DMA can repeat that image while the application prepares an update. Across the HDMI implementations reviewed, I found no demonstration of an interrupt recycling the same two row-pair slots for a whole moving frame. Our one-pair measurement therefore answers a different question from those projects’ frame rates.

## What Espressif actually ships

1. **LT8912B video player.** The Function EV Board menu offers 800×600, 1280×720, 1280×800 and 1920×1080. The BSP source also handles a programmatic 1024×768 selection. The MP4 example explicitly selects 1280×720. Its default uses two RGB888 panel buffers and passes them to the decoder. After submitting a frame the player waits on a semaphore signalled by the refresh callback. Its separate-decoder-buffer configuration instead waits for the colour transfer to finish. The default panel-buffer path requires the video dimensions to match the HDMI output. A small movie on a 720p carrier would not establish a lower HDMI timing. [Player and callback selection](https://github.com/espressif/esp-iot-solution/blob/f721a40c032f6168844fe3bd321bc182e2593348/examples/display/lcd/hdmi_video_renderer/main/main.c#L43-L103) · [Player configuration](https://github.com/espressif/esp-iot-solution/blob/f721a40c032f6168844fe3bd321bc182e2593348/examples/display/lcd/hdmi_video_renderer/main/Kconfig.projbuild) · [BSP mode table](https://github.com/espressif/esp-bsp/blob/fb5714e02cf4289d6f092f817a7ef1e0b72a49ed/bsp/esp32_p4_function_ev_board/esp32_p4_function_ev_board.c#L710-L752)
2. **Who feeds the bridge on our SDK.** In IDF 5.5.5 each panel framebuffer has a one-item DMA list with the whole frame as its transfer length. The DesignWare DMA controller supplies the fixed DSI FIFO using hardware handshaking and DMA-controlled flow. At full transfer completion the ISR reads the selected framebuffer index, marks its descriptor valid and last, and restarts DMA **before** notifying the application. If the selected buffer stays the same, scanout reads it again. A caller alternately using two buffers must track which old buffer retired. Submitting a panel-owned address can avoid a pixel copy. Completion of a drawing-buffer copy only releases its source. Neither callback identifies a retired two-row slot. The LT8912B driver delegates active-pixel delivery to this DPI panel. [Pinned DMA setup and restart](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/b774170ff46c393eeb5e495ea37936038d3f4f4f/components/esp_lcd/dsi/esp_lcd_panel_dpi.c#L72-L166) · [Frame descriptor and draw paths](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/b774170ff46c393eeb5e495ea37936038d3f4f4f/components/esp_lcd/dsi/esp_lcd_panel_dpi.c#L436-L565) · [Bridge integration](https://github.com/espressif/esp-bsp/blob/fb5714e02cf4289d6f092f817a7ef1e0b72a49ed/components/lcd/esp_lcd_lt8912b/esp_lcd_lt8912b.c#L217-L219)
3. **Two easily confused strip examples.** Espressif’s 1080p60 LT9611 HDMI dashboard uses two 20-line internal LVGL buffers. DMA2D copies changed rectangles into a persistent scanout framebuffer and the colour-transfer callback releases the LVGL buffer. The parallel RGB LCD driver really does offer interrupt-filled bounce buffers through `on_bounce_empty`. It drives a different peripheral from the P4-PC’s MIPI DSI host. The RGB driver is a conceptual precedent for alternating ownership. The HDMI dashboard shows how to reduce drawing traffic. [HDMI partial-buffer source](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/blob/ad20ded62a2d67a26e501fdc1905f43ba7ad6c81/examples/peripherals/lcd/dsi_hdmi_bridge/main/lvgl_port.c#L49-L107) · [LT9611 board and timing](https://github.com/espressif/esp-idf/tree/ad20ded62a2d67a26e501fdc1905f43ba7ad6c81/examples/peripherals/lcd/dsi_hdmi_bridge) · [Parallel RGB bounce-buffer contract](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/v5.4/esp32p4/api-reference/peripherals/lcd/rgb_lcd.html)

## What this settles for mode 20

1. **Keep the proven carrier as the timing control.** The monitor has already accepted 684×384 with the existing pattern and margins. Different carriers and HDMI metadata would introduce another variable. The distinction that matters is between the scan repeating a complete physical image and the producer having to finish future rows while the current pair is in flight.
2. **Our physical DMA retirement evidence is the closest reusable ownership precedent.** A fresh block completion and cleared VALID for the descriptor reading A established when its memory was free. B was still valid after publication and the replacement descriptor retired from A. SDK refresh callbacks, an LVGL flush acknowledgement and completion of a PPA job cannot establish those three facts.
3. **Two sustained questions remain.** At the tested timing, 38.697 µs used 60.1% of the nominal 64.4 µs pair interval. The 25.703 µs difference is headroom for that one pre-expanded pair measured from IRQ entry. Consecutive A and B reuses, interrupt delivery, real pixel preparation, the worst timing under drawing load and the handoff across frame end remain unmeasured. There is a separate framing constraint. A full 384-line frame would have 192 two-row transfers. The rev-1.3 bridge’s DMA-controlled block-count field has four bits and the reviewed contract defines no encoding for 192. The proven fixture used four descriptors. The SDK’s full-frame and the external applications avoid that question entirely. [Archived hardware-contract review](../SCAN-001/S03-FEASIBILITY.md) · [Physical mode 20 result](NURPLES-SCROLL-JUDDER.md#olimex-mode-20-one-frame-result)

This research leaves the stock single logical framebuffer and the accepted HDMI timing in place. No source reviewed supplies evidence that justifies changing either.
